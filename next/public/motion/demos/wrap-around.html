A vase, by hand.

Every Ash vase starts as a lump of grey clay and a slow wheel. Mara pulls the walls up by hand, twice, until the rim sits thin and level. It dries for six days. Then it is fired, glazed and fired again, so the surface keeps a little of the kiln in it. No two come out the same. That is the point. Some run a shade warmer. Some carry a faint ripple where a thumb rested. We photograph each one on a plain table, in daylight, and show it exactly as it is. It ships wrapped in paper, in a box that folds flat afterwards. If one arrives with a chip, tell us. A new one leaves within five days. Each is numbered underneath, in pencil. Pick it up. Put it where the light is good.
