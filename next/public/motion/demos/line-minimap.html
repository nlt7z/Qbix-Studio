Case study · Forma

A shop for clay. Built in three weeks.

Forma makes ceramics in a studio of four. Qbix designed and built the shop that sells them, from the first brief to a live domain.

The brief

Forma's site was three years old and took orders by email. They wanted one thing: a page that looks like the work, and a shop a customer can finish in two minutes.

We read 200 past orders before drawing anything. Nearly all began with a photo, so the photos became the structure.

Scope was fixed at one week of design and two of build.

Moodboard to palette

We pinned 30 photographs of raw clay, bone china and wet glaze to one board, then cut it to six.

The palette came from the shelf, not a swatch book: chalk, clay, a pale celadon and ink. Nothing else gets a colour.

Type and grid

One typeface, set large and tight. A 12-column grid holds a product, its price and its care note without a box around any of them.

Every photo is cropped to the same ratio, so a shelf of 40 pieces reads as one calm wall.

Components

Cards, buttons and form fields are components with tokens behind them. There are 41, each drawn once and used wherever it fits.

A product card has four states. Hover shows the second photo, and the price never moves.

Strata keeps the tokens and the code in step, so a colour changed once changes everywhere.

Build and preview

Pages are built in Next.js and sent to a preview link on every change. Forma's team reviewed on their phones, between kilns.

Two review rounds, 19 comments, each one closed within a day.

Launch

The shop went live on forma.studio on a Tuesday, 21 days after the first call. Orders in the first month ran 38% above the month before.

Qbix still looks after it. A new drop is a new page and an hour of work.
